How to use this playbook: work through it in order. Each step builds on the one before. Most education brands we audit skip steps 1, 2 and 7, which is why their cost per lead looks fine but enrolments don't follow.

1Know your numbers before you spend

Before you set a budget, work out what you can afford to pay for a lead. You need three numbers from your own records:

  • Revenue per enrolled student (your fee plus any commission)
  • Lead-to-counselling rate: what share of leads attend a counselling session
  • Counselling-to-enrolment rate: what share of those enrol

Break-even cost per lead = revenue per student × lead-to-enrolment rate.

Example (made-up numbers)Value
Revenue per enrolled student₹60,000
Leads that enrol5 in every 100 (5%)
Break-even cost per lead₹60,000 × 5% = ₹3,000
Target (marketing = one third of revenue)about ₹1,000 per lead

Write your own numbers down. Every decision later in this playbook uses them.

2Set up tracking that tells Meta who your good leads are

Meta's algorithm learns from the events you send it. If you only send “lead submitted”, it finds people who like filling in forms, not people who enrol.

  • Install the Meta Pixel on every page of your website.
  • Set up the Conversions API (server-side tracking) so leads are still counted when browsers block the Pixel.
  • Verify your domain in Meta Business Manager.
  • Send deeper events back to Meta from your CRM: counselling booked, application submitted, enrolled.
  • Capture UTM tags and the click ID (fbclid) on every form so you know which ad each lead came from.
Why it matters: once Meta receives “counselling booked” events (aim for at least 50 a week across your account), you can optimise campaigns for that event instead of raw leads. Lead quality usually improves noticeably.

3Build a simple campaign structure

Complicated accounts with dozens of tiny ad sets starve Meta of data. Keep it simple:

CampaignPurposeBudget share
ProspectingReach new students. Broad targeting by location and age; let creative do the targeting.70–80%
RetargetingWebsite visitors, video viewers and people who opened your form but didn't submit.10–20%
TestingNew offers and creative angles before they go into prospecting.~10%

Split campaigns by destination or programme only when the offer, landing page or economics are genuinely different (for example UK postgraduate vs Canada undergraduate).

4Offers that attract serious students

“Free counselling” is what everyone offers, so it attracts everyone. A specific offer filters for intent:

  • “Get your personalised UK university shortlist in 24 hours”
  • “Free profile evaluation for MS in Germany: find out if you qualify”
  • “Free IELTS mock test with a band score report”
  • “Scholarship check: see which scholarships match your profile”
  • “Visa document checklist for Canada: sent on WhatsApp”
Quality filter: add one qualifying question to your form, such as intended intake or budget range. You'll get slightly fewer leads, but your counsellors will spend their time on the right ones.

5Creatives: the 3-angle rule

Run at least three genuinely different creatives in every ad set. Colour variations of the same poster count as one.

AngleExample
Problem“Rejected by 3 universities? Here's what your profile is missing.”
Proof20-second phone video of a real student talking about their visa approval or first day on campus.
Offer“Free course shortlist on WhatsApp within 24 hours. Tap to start.”
  • Make vertical (9:16) video first; it works across Reels, Stories and Feed.
  • Put the hook in the first three seconds and add captions; most people watch muted.
  • Replace the weakest creative every 2–4 weeks, or when frequency passes about 3–4 per week.
  • Real students and real counsellors beat stock photos almost every time.

6Lead forms vs landing pages

Meta instant formsYour own landing page
Cost per leadUsually lowerUsually higher
Lead qualityOften lower; many forget they enquiredUsually higher; more effort means more intent
Best forLarge, simple offers; testing demandHigher-value programmes; when sales capacity is limited

If you use instant forms, choose the “higher intent” form type, add a qualifying question, and add a short context screen that explains exactly what happens next.

7Follow-up: where enrolments are won or lost

The best ads in the world can't save slow follow-up. Students enquire with several consultancies at once; the first useful reply often wins.

  • Send an automatic WhatsApp message within one minute of every lead.
  • Call within 15 minutes during working hours.
  • Make at least 5 contact attempts over 7 days (call, WhatsApp, email) before marking a lead dead.
  • Offer a booking link so students can choose a counselling slot themselves.
  • Send reminders the day before and an hour before every session.
  • Record every lead's status in one place (CRM or shared sheet).

8The weekly review routine

One hour a week, same day every week:

  1. Check cost per lead, cost per counselling session and cost per enrolment by campaign.
  2. Ask counsellors which campaigns brought the best and worst leads this week.
  3. Pause the weakest creative; launch one new angle.
  4. Move budget towards campaigns with the lowest cost per counselling session, not just per lead.
  5. Log what you changed and why.

Change budgets gradually (around 20% at a time) so campaigns don't restart their learning.

9Season planning by intake

Demand follows intake deadlines. Start campaigns well before application deadlines, and confirm dates with each university, as they change.

10Launch checklist

  • Break-even and target cost per lead written down
  • Pixel and Conversions API installed and tested
  • Domain verified; key events set up
  • UTM tags and click IDs captured on every form
  • Specific offer chosen, with one qualifying question
  • 3 different creative angles ready, vertical video first
  • Landing page or instant form matched to the ad's promise
  • Automatic WhatsApp reply within one minute
  • Call-back process and 5-attempt follow-up agreed with the team
  • CRM or lead sheet ready, with lead status columns
  • Weekly review time in the calendar
